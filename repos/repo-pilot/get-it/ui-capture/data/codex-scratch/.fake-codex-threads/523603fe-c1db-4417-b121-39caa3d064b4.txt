1. Pick AT MOST 4 concepts per page. Quality over quantity. Skip pages with

   Judge each page independently, exactly as if it were the only page given.
2. For EVERY concept, set "page" to the integer from the "===== PAGE_INDEX n
   =====" marker of the page the concept appears on.
3. Each concept's "anchor" MUST be a verbatim copy of the LAST 30–80
   characters of the sentence that introduces it, taken EXACTLY from THAT
   page's text (no paraphrase). The tag pill will be planted right after
   this anchor — so the anchor string MUST appear once on its page, and the
   renderer anchors at its tail. Pick anchors that are unique on the page.
4. LANGUAGE: detect the language of each page's text and write BOTH "label"
   and "context" in that same language so they read naturally next to the
   source. Italian page → Italian outputs, English page → English outputs,
   etc. Never translate the source.
5. "label" is what shows on the pill (≤ 35 chars). Make it a short noun
   phrase the reader would skim and instantly recognise.
6. "context" gives the visualizer everything it needs to render the concept
   without re-reading the full page: include the concept name, key
   parameters mentioned in the text, and the field of study. 1–3 sentences.
7. Avoid trivial picks (page numbers, headings, generic phrases). Avoid
   duplicates of the same concept across a page.

Cell Biology — Lecture 7: Membrane
Transport
The plasma membrane as a barrier
The plasma membrane is a   phospholipid bilayer   about 5 nm thick with embedded proteins,
cholesterol and glycolipids — the fluid mosaic model of Singer and Nicolson (1972). Its
hydrophobic core lets small non-polar molecules such as O2, CO2 and steroid hormones cross
freely, but blocks ions and large polar molecules like glucose. Everything else needs a protein.
Passive transport
Passive transport always moves solutes   down   their electrochemical gradient and costs no ATP.
•   Simple diffusion : directly through the lipid bilayer. Rate is proportional to the concentration
difference (Fick's law: J = -D dC/dx).
•   Facilitated diffusion through channel proteins : water-filled pores, very fast (up to 10^8
ions per second), often gated by voltage, ligands or mechanical stretch. Aquaporins are
channels for water.
•   Facilitated diffusion through carrier proteins : the carrier binds the solute and changes
shape. Because the number of carriers is finite, the rate   saturates   — it follows Michaelis-
Menten-like kinetics with a Vmax and a Km. GLUT transporters for glucose are the classic
example.
Osmosis
Water moves across a semipermeable membrane from low solute concentration to high solute
concentration. A red blood cell placed in a   hypotonic   solution swells and can lyse; in a
hypertonic   solution it shrinks (crenation); in an   isotonic   solution (0.9% NaCl) it keeps its
shape. Osmotic pressure is given by the van 't Hoff relation, pi = iCRT.
Active transport
Primary active transport   uses ATP directly. The   Na+/K+-ATPase   pumps 3 Na+ out and 2 K+
in per ATP and consumes up to a third of a resting cell's energy. Other P-type pumps include
the Ca2+-ATPase of the sarcoplasmic reticulum and the H+/K+-ATPase in the stomach (the
target of omeprazole).
Secondary active transport   uses the gradient created by a primary pump. In the small
intestine the   SGLT1 symporter   carries two Na+ ions down their gradient and one glucose
molecule up its gradient into the epithelial cell. Glucose then leaves the cell on the blood side
through GLUT2 by facilitated diffusion. Antiporters work the same way but move the two

Bulk transport
Large particles are moved by vesicles.
1.   Endocytosis : phagocytosis (cell eating — e.g. macrophages engulfing bacteria),
pinocytosis (cell drinking) and   receptor-mediated endocytosis   through clathrin-coated
pits, which is how cells take up LDL cholesterol.
2.   Exocytosis : secretory vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane, releasing hormones,
enzymes or neurotransmitters.
Clinical links
Cystic fibrosis is caused by mutations in CFTR, a chloride channel. Without functional CFTR,
chloride secretion fails and water does not follow, leaving thick mucus in the lungs and
pancreas. Familial hypercholesterolaemia is caused by defective LDL receptors, which block
receptor-mediated endocytosis and raise plasma cholesterol.
