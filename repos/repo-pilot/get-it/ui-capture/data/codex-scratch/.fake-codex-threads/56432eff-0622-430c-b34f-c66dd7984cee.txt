3. Each concept's "anchor" MUST be a verbatim copy of the LAST 30–80

   page's text (no paraphrase). The tag pill will be planted right after
   this anchor — so the anchor string MUST appear once on its page, and the
   renderer anchors at its tail. Pick anchors that are unique on the page.
4. LANGUAGE: detect the language of each page's text and write BOTH "label"

6. "context" gives the visualizer everything it needs to render the concept
   without re-reading the full page: include the concept name, key
   parameters mentioned in the text, and the field of study. 1–3 sentences.
7. Avoid trivial picks (page numbers, headings, generic phrases). Avoid

Thermodynamics — Chapter 4: Entropy and
the Second Law
Why a second law?
The first law says energy is conserved: dU = dQ - dW. It allows a cold cup of coffee to
spontaneously heat up by cooling the room, as long as the energy balances. It never happens.
The   second law   tells us which direction processes go.
Two classical statements, which can be shown to be equivalent:
•   Kelvin-Planck : no cyclic device can convert heat from a single reservoir entirely into work.
•   Clausius : heat does not flow spontaneously from a colder body to a hotter body.
Heat engines and the Carnot limit
A heat engine takes heat Qh from a hot reservoir at Th, produces work W, and rejects Qc to a
cold reservoir at Tc. Its efficiency is eta = W / Qh = 1 - Qc / Qh.
The most efficient possible engine operating between two temperatures is the reversible
Carnot cycle : two isothermal and two adiabatic steps. Its efficiency depends only on the
temperatures (in kelvin):
eta_Carnot = 1 - Tc / Th.
A power plant with steam at 600 K rejecting to a river at 300 K can never exceed 50%
efficiency, no matter how good the engineering. Real plants reach 35-45%.
Refrigerators and heat pumps run the cycle backwards. Their performance is measured by the
coefficient of performance, COP = Qc / W for a fridge.
Entropy
Clausius defined a new state function,   entropy , through its change along a reversible path:
dS = dQ_rev / T.
Because S is a state function, the change between two states is the same for any path, even
irreversible ones — we just have to compute it along an imagined reversible path. For an ideal
gas: Delta S = n Cv ln(T2/T1) + n R ln(V2/V1).
The second law in its most compact form: for an isolated system,   Delta S >= 0 . The equality
holds only for reversible processes; every real (irreversible) process generates entropy. Friction,
heat transfer across a finite temperature difference, free expansion and mixing are all sources

of entropy generation.
Statistical interpretation
Boltzmann connected entropy to the number of microscopic arrangements W compatible with a
macroscopic state:
S = k_B ln W
where k_B = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K. A gas spreading through a container is not "forced" to spread:
there are overwhelmingly more microstates with the gas spread out than with the gas bunched
in one corner. The arrow of time is a statement about probability.
Free energy and spontaneity
At constant temperature and pressure — the conditions of most chemistry and biology — the
useful criterion is the   Gibbs free energy , G = H - TS. A process is spontaneous when Delta G
< 0. This is why ATP hydrolysis (Delta G about -30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions) can
drive unfavourable reactions in the cell when the two are coupled.
Third law
The entropy of a perfect crystal approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero.
A consequence is that 0 K can never actually be reached in a finite number of steps.
