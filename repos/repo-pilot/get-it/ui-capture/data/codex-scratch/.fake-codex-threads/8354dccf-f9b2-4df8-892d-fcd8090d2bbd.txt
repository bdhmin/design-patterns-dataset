  • "2d-anim" — physical processes, simulations, mechanisms in motion

RULES
1. Pick AT MOST 4 concepts per page. Quality over quantity. Skip pages with
   no good visualization candidates — simply emit no concepts for them.
   Judge each page independently, exactly as if it were the only page given.
2. For EVERY concept, set "page" to the integer from the "===== PAGE_INDEX n
   =====" marker of the page the concept appears on.
3. Each concept's "anchor" MUST be a verbatim copy of the LAST 30–80
   characters of the sentence that introduces it, taken EXACTLY from THAT
   page's text (no paraphrase). The tag pill will be planted right after
   this anchor — so the anchor string MUST appear once on its page, and the
   renderer anchors at its tail. Pick anchors that are unique on the page.
4. LANGUAGE: detect the language of each page's text and write BOTH "label"
   and "context" in that same language so they read naturally next to the
   source. Italian page → Italian outputs, English page → English outputs,
   etc. Never translate the source.
5. "label" is what shows on the pill (≤ 35 chars). Make it a short noun
   phrase the reader would skim and instantly recognise.
6. "context" gives the visualizer everything it needs to render the concept
   without re-reading the full page: include the concept name, key
   parameters mentioned in the text, and the field of study. 1–3 sentences.
7. Avoid trivial picks (page numbers, headings, generic phrases). Avoid
   duplicates of the same concept across a page.

Linear Algebra — Eigenvalues,
Eigenvectors and Diagonalization
Definition
Let A be an n x n matrix. A non-zero vector v is an   eigenvector   of A with   eigenvalue   lambda if
A v = lambda v.
Geometrically, A does not change the direction of v: it only stretches it by a factor lambda (or
flips it if lambda is negative). Every other vector gets rotated off its line.
Finding eigenvalues
A v = lambda v means (A - lambda I) v = 0 has a non-zero solution, which happens exactly
when A - lambda I is singular:
det(A - lambda I) = 0.
This is the   characteristic equation . Its left side is a polynomial of degree n in lambda, the
characteristic polynomial, so A has at most n eigenvalues (counting complex ones and
multiplicity).
Worked example.   A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]]. Then det(A - lambda I) = (2 - lambda)^2 - 1 = lambda^2 -
4 lambda + 3 = (lambda - 1)(lambda - 3). So lambda = 1 and lambda = 3. For lambda = 3: (A -
3I)v = 0 gives v = (1, 1). For lambda = 1: v = (1, -1).
Useful checks: the sum of the eigenvalues equals the   trace   of A (2 + 2 = 4 = 1 + 3) and their
product equals the   determinant   (4 - 1 = 3 = 1 x 3).
Algebraic and geometric multiplicity
The algebraic multiplicity of lambda is how many times it appears as a root of the characteristic
polynomial. The geometric multiplicity is the dimension of its eigenspace, the null space of A -
lambda I. Always: 1 <= geometric <= algebraic. When the geometric multiplicity is strictly
smaller the matrix is called   defective . Example: [[1, 1], [0, 1]] has lambda = 1 twice but only
one independent eigenvector.
Diagonalization
A is   diagonalizable   if there is an invertible matrix P such that
A = P D P^(-1)

===== PAGE_INDEX 1 =====
with D diagonal. The columns of P are eigenvectors and the diagonal entries of D are the
matching eigenvalues. A is diagonalizable if and only if it has n linearly independent
eigenvectors. A sufficient condition: n distinct eigenvalues.
Why we care: powers become trivial. A^k = P D^k P^(-1), and D^k just raises each diagonal
entry to the k. This makes long-run behaviour of systems like x(k+1) = A x(k) easy to read: the
component along the eigenvector with the largest |lambda| dominates.
Symmetric matrices — the spectral theorem
If A is real and symmetric (A = A^T) then all eigenvalues are real, eigenvectors of distinct
eigenvalues are orthogonal, and A can always be diagonalized by an   orthogonal   matrix Q: A =
Q D Q^T. Covariance matrices are symmetric, which is why principal component analysis
(PCA) works: the eigenvectors are the principal directions and the eigenvalues are the
variances along them.
Applications seen in class
•   Markov chains : the steady state of a transition matrix is the eigenvector with eigenvalue 1.
•   Google PageRank : the ranking is the dominant eigenvector of the link matrix, found by the
power method.
•   Systems of ODEs : x' = A x has solutions of the form e^(lambda t) v; negative real parts
mean the system is stable.
•   Vibrations : the natural frequencies of a mass-spring system are the square roots of the
eigenvalues of the stiffness matrix.
